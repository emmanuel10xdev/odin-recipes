Nigerian Beans Porridge (Ewa Alagbopo / Ewa Riro)

Nigerian beans porridge is a rich, comforting, and deeply savory dish made with brown or black-eyed beans, slow-cooked in a luscious palm oil broth infused with traditional aromatics and ground crayfish.

It is known for its tender texture, naturally sweet undertone, and thick, deeply satisfying stew-like consistency.

To prepare it, the beans are parboiled and simmered until soft, then combined with a rich base of onions, blended peppers, palm oil, and seasoning cubes until everything melts into a creamy porridge.

Optional additions like smoked fish, kpomo, fried plantain (dodo), or sweet potatoes can be stirred in or served on top to elevate its texture and hearty flavor.

It is best served warm and can be enjoyed thoroughly on its own or alongside freshly fried plantain, soaked garri, or soft agege bread.

Ingredients

  • Brown beans or black-eyed beans
  • Water
  • Onions
  • Palm oil
  • Fresh pepper
  • Tomatoes (optional)
  • Crayfish, ground (optional)
  • Seasoning cubes
  • Salt
  • Smoked fish or dried fish (optional)

How to Make Beans Porridge

  1. Pick through the beans and remove stones, dirt, and damaged beans.
  2. Wash the beans thoroughly and place them in a pot with enough water.
  3. Boil the beans until they become soft and tender, adding more water when necessary.
  4. Chop the onions and fresh pepper while the beans are cooking.
  5. Once the beans are soft, add the onions, pepper, and tomatoes if using.
  6. Add palm oil, seasoning cubes, and crayfish, then stir thoroughly.
  7. Add smoked or dried fish if desired and allow the beans to simmer for a few minutes.
  8. Add salt to taste and continue cooking until the beans become thick and flavorful.
  9. Taste and adjust the seasoning if necessary.
  10. Serve hot with your preferred side, such as fried plantain, bread, or rice.
